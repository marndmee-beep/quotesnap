The lines that protect your margin

  • Materials. List them separately. Materials are usually 40 to 50 percent of a job's value, and lumping them into one price hides what you paid.
  • Labour. Give hours or days and a rate, so the customer sees what work costs rather than guessing.
  • Permits and inspections. Put them on their own line. They are a cost you pass through, not part of your labour.
  • Equipment hire and waste removal. Easy to forget and painful to absorb at the end of a job.
  • Contingency. For jobs where you cannot see everything yet, add an allowance and label it clearly.

Estimate or quote?

An estimate is a rough figure that can change once work starts. A quote is a fixed price for the job as described. Say which one you are giving, and if it is a quote, write down what it assumes, such as "assumes existing joists are sound".

What to include

  • Your business name, address and contact details.
  • The site address, not just the customer's home if they differ.
  • Scope, itemised, with the exclusions written down.
  • When the price expires. Two to four weeks is normal.
  • Deposit and payment schedule for larger jobs.
