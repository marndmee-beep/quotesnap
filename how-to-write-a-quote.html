How to write a quote that gets approved

Most quotes that lose aren't too expensive. They're too vague. Here's what to put in yours.

1. Itemise the work

One lump sum gives the customer nothing to judge. Break the job into lines: strip out the old unit, supply and fit a new one, make good the wall. Itemised quotes are easier to compare, easier to approve, and they protect you when the customer asks for changes partway through.

2. Write down your assumptions

Every quote rests on something you have assumed. Put it in writing: access is available during working hours, the customer supplies the tiles, the existing wiring is up to code. If an assumption later proves wrong, you have grounds to discuss a variation rather than absorbing the cost.

3. Separate what's included from what isn't

Exclusions stop arguments. Waste removal, decorating after a repair, and making good are the usual ones people assume are included. Say plainly what you are not doing.

4. Say how long the price holds

Materials move. Write "price held for 14 days" and you give the customer a reason to decide without pushing them. It also protects you from quoting a price in March and doing the work in June at the same number.

5. State your payment terms

For anything sizeable, ask for a deposit and set out the schedule, such as 30 percent to book and the balance on completion. Agreeing this before work starts is far easier than asking later.

6. Make the total easy to find

People scan. Put one clear total at the bottom, with the breakdown above it. Tax, if you charge it, should be its own line rather than mixed into the price.

7. Send it the same day

Speed wins work. A quote that arrives while you're still the person they remember beats a slightly better one that turns up next week. That's the main reason to quote from your phone, before you've left the drive.
